# Getting your families on NestNote (for Sitters)

If you babysit through NestNote, you already know how much smoother a gig feels when the important details live in one place—not scattered across texts, camera rolls, and sticky notes. This short guide helps you explain that to parents and nudge them toward actually using the app with you.

---

## Why families benefit (and why they’ll thank you)

**Less back-and-forth on sit night.** Alarm codes, Wi‑Fi passwords, where the spare key is, which kid is allergic to what, and “the real” bedtime routine are easy to forget in a rush. When a family keeps that in their **nest**—NestNote’s shared space for their household info—you’re not guessing or digging through old messages.

**Instructions and routines stay consistent.** Families can add **entries** (notes with the details you need), **places** (where things are or where to go), **routines** (step-by-step checklists for bedtime, meals, or handoff), and **contacts** (pediatrician, neighbors, grandparents). You see what they intend you to see for **your session**, instead of a wall of private family notes.

**One source of truth when plans change.** If something updates after they invited you, they can fix it in the nest; you’re not working off three different screenshots from last month.

**Optional backup for non-app users.** From a session, nest owners can **export session information as a PDF** to print or share—handy if someone in the household prefers paper or email.

---

## How easy is it for parents? Very.

The family’s side is built around **sessions**—the block of time you’re sitting—and an **invite**:

1. They create a session with the dates and times.
2. They invite their sitter (you). That produces an **invite code** you use in the app to join the session and see what they’ve shared for that booking.
3. They can pull from what they’ve already saved in their nest (codes, Wi‑Fi, routines, etc.) so they’re not retyping everything each time.

You don’t need to micromanage their setup. The pitch is simple: *“If you put the basics in NestNote once, every future sit is easier—for you and for me.”*

---

## You can start the ball rolling: session requests

If you already use NestNote as a Sitter, you don’t have to wait for them to think of it. You can create a **session request** and send it to a **nest owner**. They get an invite code, can accept your request, and adjust dates and times if needed—so you’re not stuck playing calendar ping‑pong in three apps.

In the app, the idea is spelled out directly: *send the request to a nest owner; they receive an invite code and can accept or tweak the session.*

That’s a concrete ask you can text a parent: *“I use NestNote for sitting—can I send you a session request so we’re aligned on times and you can drop codes and routines in one place?”*

---

## What to say to a family (copy-friendly)

Use whatever tone fits your relationship; these are starters:

- **Short:** “I use NestNote for sitting. It’s one place for your codes, Wi‑Fi, and routines so I’m not texting you at 7:58. Want to add me for our next session?”
- **Helpful:** “You only set up the nest once; after that it’s mostly picking session dates and inviting me with a code. I join with the code and I’m good.”
- **If they’re busy:** “If you want, I can send a session request from my side—you just accept and adjust the times if needed.”

## Extra topics worth mentioning

| Topic | Why it helps |
|--------|----------------|
| **Privacy and boundaries** | Access is organized around **sessions**; families choose what to include for each sit so you’re not hunting through unrelated household notes. |
| **Multiple families** | You can work with several nests; each family keeps their own information organized. |
| **Fewer “sorry, what was the code again?” texts** | Parents often underestimate how often they repeat the same five facts; NestNote makes that repetition unnecessary. |
| **Emergencies** | Having contacts and clear instructions in one structured place is useful when something unexpected happens—not just for convenience. |

---

## Quick recap

- **Nest** = the family’s stash of household and care info.  
- **Session** = your specific sitting job; **invite code** = how you join it in the app.  
- **Session request** = your way to propose dates and pull a hesitant family into the flow.  
- **PDF export** = optional print/email summary from the family’s session if someone wants off-app backup.

You’re not selling software—you’re offering a calmer handoff and fewer mistakes. NestNote is just the tool that makes that realistic.
